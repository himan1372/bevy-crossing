THE ROAD TO THE FULL GAME.

What works in development builds today, what is being built, and what comes next. Bevy Crossing rebuilds the engine in Bevy; all game content loads at runtime from your own Animal Crossing GameCube disc.

Latest work and next steps

Implemented: the police box. Walk into the police box door (facing up-left) and Booker greets you. Lost items sit on the display tables; face one and press E, and he'll offer to give it to you. A new lost and found starts with one furniture item and two shirts, as in the original.

House upgrades: Once your loan is paid off, walk into Nook's shop and he'll offer to remodel; pick a roof colour and the next day (after restarting or loading) your house is bigger outside with the new roof, and Nook bills you for it. Inside, the room grows too (8, then 10 units across), keeping your furniture where it was. With a basement or the second floor, walk down into the stairs beside the front door to change floors; each floor keeps its own furniture. Pay off the last loan and Nook builds your statue in front of the train station.

Post office: Walk up to the post office door facing up-right and press E to go in. Pelly (day) or Phyllis (night) works the counter. If you owe on your house she offers to take a payment: choose the amount digit by digit in the original repay window; bells bags in your pockets count too, and you get change. Your current save starts with no debt, so you'll see repaying once house upgrades let you order an expansion; a new game starts owing 17,400.

Idle clock: Stand still in town and, after a moment, the original's clock window fades in at the lower right with the date, weekday and time; its digits flip when the minute changes, and it fades away when you walk or talk. Nook's shop no longer shows the raffle-day decorations every day (they appear only on the last day of the month, as in the original), and the small marks above text in the pockets screen are gone.

Shop wall clock: Inside Nook's shop, the clock on the back wall shows the real time, swings its pendulum and ticks, as in the original. The shop's key-hint line is now part of the F10 development text.

Moving second texture layers: Models that blend two textures now draw both, and the second one can scroll on its own: the sprinkler's spray flows and the lawnmower's cut grass shimmers as in the original.

Retail-clean screen: The original game shows no on-screen HUD in town or at home, so the port's development text (bells and pockets, tool, clock, house key hints, quest line) is now hidden by default. Press F10 to show it again while playtesting. In the original you read the time from the clocks in buildings and in your house, and your bells in the pockets screen.

Lawnmower: On the backyard-lawn or daisy-meadow carpet, push the lawnmower from behind (hold G and walk into it from its back) and cut grass appears under it, with the mower's own sound on each push. On other carpets, or pushed from another side, it moves like any furniture.

Sprinkler: Press E beside it to turn the water on or off; the spray fades in and out over a moment with the original switch sounds, and its running sound plays while it is on. As in the original, a press while the water is still fading is ignored by the sprinkler itself (only the switch flips), and leaving the room saves the sprinkler's real state. The water's second texture layer now scrolls, so the spray flows.

Cuckoo clock: On the hour the bird calls once per hour (12 at noon and midnight), and once on the half hour, each call replaying its original animation with its sound; its hour and minute hands follow the game clock. Visiting during minute 0 or 30 starts the calls, as in the original.

Room insects: All forty insects can now be placed. The cicadas, crickets, mole cricket, mosquito and frog run the original room-insect sound scheduler and chirp from their cage when it says so; cicada and cricket cages replay their animation with each chirp. The firefly cage pulses its light the original way. Faithful to the original, they are usually quiet: the game's room "beat" depends on an audio callback it never installs, so a placed insect only sounds when all six sound channels are busy at the moment it appears. That behaviour is reproduced, not a missing feature.

Slot machine: press E beside it to run its original one-shot lever/reel animation and positional sound. E during a spin does not restart it; once stopped, either switch direction starts another spin. It uses the original two reel textures and timing. This furniture has no payout or wallet action. Saved placement/switches survive load; a spin is transient and does not replay on load. Philip visual/audio acceptance is pending.

Boxing bell: press E beside it to strike the bell with its original animation and positional sound. With ringside-seating wallpaper, a strike also starts four seconds of camera sparkles, an extra sound and small light pulses. Rapid E presses during the strike do not restart it. Moving, turning, pickup and saved room state are preserved; flashes pause and clear on load or room exit. Philip visual/audio acceptance is pending.

Electric room fan: place the electric fan from pockets, then press E beside it to switch it on or off. It oscillates and animates its blades with the original disc rig and textures, eases up to speed and coasts to a stop. Power follows movement and turning and survives F5/F9; pickup returns the same fan. Original switch sounds and positional motor sound use the existing mixer. This is separate from the handheld summer fans; Philip visual/audio acceptance is pending.

Implemented: shiny surfaces and balloons. The renderer now reproduces the original reflection mapping (G_TEXTURE_GEN): shiny models take their texture from the direction each surface faces relative to the camera, as the GameCube's emu64 does. All eight balloons can now be placed in your house; they float above their weights, sway at the original speed, and E gives them the source's switch nudge. Fish-tank and insect-cage glass and the clock faces checked so far now shine the original way; mirrors, some cabinets and statues use the same path but have not been compared yet. Balloon strings stay invisible: their faint alpha falls below emu64's edge threshold, so the original cuts them the same way.

Try it: place a balloon from pockets; press E beside it to make it bob faster for a moment.

Fish and insects: All forty fish and 34 of the forty insects can be placed from pockets ("Place in room") as their original aquariums and cages, read from your disc. They animate at the source's half speed, can be pushed, pulled and turned, survive F5/F9, and pick up as exactly the same fish or insect. The sound and glow insects and the frog followed later (see above).

Try it: catch a fish or bug, enter your house, press I and choose Place in room. Some larger tanks take two floor cells.

Window sunshine: Your house's two side windows now cast the original light patches on the floor, with a soft light shaft from each window. They are read from your disc and follow the source: the right window lights the morning and evening, the left one the afternoon and the hours after midnight, growing and shrinking with the time of day; daylight is pale blue-white and night light is the yellow moonlight colour. Switching the room light on fades them almost away, off brings them back, and they briefly vanish around noon and midnight as in the original.

Try it: enter your house in the afternoon (left patch) or press F6 to reach the evening (right patch, moonlight); press Z to watch them fade with the light switch. Rain and snow would dim them further, but this port has no weather yet.

Room light switch: In your house, Z switches the room light on and off as on the GameCube, with the original switch sounds and the 20-frame flicker when it comes on. The light's level follows the source: it brightens the floor and walls through the room colour, adds the room's ceiling light to furniture and the player from each object's position, and dims the sun and moonlight while it is on. Turned off at night, the room falls back to dark moonlight.

Try it: enter your house and press Z. At night (F6 advances the clock an hour) the difference is large; in daylight switching on mostly warms the room. The switch is not saved, as in the original: it stays as you left it while you play, and starting or loading (F9) resets it to on from 18:00 to 05:00 and off otherwise. Lamps still do not switch or glow on their own; the original game has no per-lamp light.

Clocks: ten clocks show the local game time with source hand angles and half-speed disc animation; the antique clock ticks on the shared room beat. No configurable alarm exists in the audited callbacks.

TVs and lamps: five TVs support E power, animated screens, positional audio and saved switches; power follows movement and turning. Missing static model parts are restored for four lamps; the original lamps are not separate light sources.

Monthly diary: face a diary on a table or box and press E. Left/Right changes month; Enter edits; Ctrl+Enter finishes for Yes / Rewrite confirmation. Esc discards a draft or closes the reader. F5 after closing saves the shared twelve-month notebook. Full retail calendar, virtual keyboard and other-player locks remain follow-ups.

Also implemented: the original music grid with M marking and ordered partial take-out, panel/cloth motion, front access and Start / Stop, plus disc-fed device filters/hiss. Eight fans and eight pinwheels can be held and used; F1 in the home grants toys when two pockets are empty.
